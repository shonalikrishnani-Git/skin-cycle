Does skin change across the menstrual cycle?

AI-assisted rapid review Following Cochrane rapid-review guidance. Directed by Sonali Krishnani; the protocol, screening, extraction and ratings were done by AI agents (Claude). No human second reviewer; not peer-reviewed; not medical advice.

Question 1In people with natural cycles, does facial skin differ between cycle phases?

Question 2Has anyone tested whether timing skincare to the cycle beats a steady routine?

  • 484records screened from PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, Consensus and reference lists
  • 47studies included
  • κ 0.87agreement between two independent AI screeners (97%)
  • 0studies or registered trials of cycle-timed vs steady skincare

Answer to question 1

Only acne shows a fairly consistent pattern: many people with acne say it flares around their period, most often the week before. For oil, hydration, barrier, sensitivity, colour and skin bacteria, the studies are small and conflicting, and measured changes are small.

Answer to question 2

It has never been tested. No study or registered trial compares cycle-timed skincare with a steady routine. Untested isn't the same as harmful, but it isn't evidence either.

Method

  • Protocol first: question, eligibility, sources and analysis were written down before any screening, with a log of every deviation.
  • Search with life-science connectors: PubMed (473 records), ClinicalTrials.gov, Consensus and medRxiv, plus reference lists of key papers.
  • Two independent AI screeners: separate AI agents each judged all 484 records without seeing the other's decisions (97.1% agreement, Cohen's κ 0.87). A third AI agent settled the 26 undecided records.
  • Two independent AI extractors: separate AI agents each recorded design, size, how phase was measured, results and risk of bias for all 47 studies. They agreed on the direction of results 90% of the time; a third AI agent checked every difference against the paper.
  • Certainty: GRADE per outcome, drafted by an AI agent.
  • Sonali's role: asked for the review, set the evidence rules the app follows, and decided what the app should and shouldn't say based on the findings. She did not screen or extract studies herself.

What this changed in the app

  • Phase headlines about sensitivity and hydration now start "In one small study…", because only one study each supports them.
  • The acne headline stays: it's the one finding with consistent support.
  • The app's core message, "timing tips, not rules", is backed by the finding that cycle-timed skincare has never been tested.
  • Earlier checks the same day cut overstatements, fixed two wrong citations, and gave every tip at least one source.

Limits

  • AI agents screened and extracted; there was no human second reviewer.
  • 33 of 47 studies could only be read as abstracts; only 4 confirmed cycle phase with hormone tests, and only 3 excluded people on the pill.
  • 71 non-English records were excluded; Consensus was capped at 3 results per search; preprints were effectively unsearched.
  • The two screeners briefly shared a scratch file; this is logged in the protocol.
