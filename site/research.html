Skin across the menstrual cycle: what the evidence says

AI-assisted desk review Sources were found and summarised by AI research agents and checked against the original papers. Sonali Krishnani set the rules and made the calls. Not a systematic review, not peer-reviewed, not medical advice.

Question

What does published evidence say about how facial skin changes across the menstrual cycle, and does it support changing a skincare routine by cycle phase?

Method

  • Sources: PubMed/PMC studies and reviews, plus public health and dermatology guidance (AAD, NHS, HSE, DermNet, FDA, LactMed).
  • AI plus checking: AI research agents found and summarised sources. Every claim was treated as wrong until a source someone had actually opened supported it.
  • Checks: three rounds (13, 14 and 17 Sep 2026), then a wording and sourcing check on 27 Sep 2026, when the key studies were read again for this review.
  • Sonali’s role: set the rules (ingredient types, not brands; a caution on every remedy; nothing goes in without a source) and decided what to keep and cut.
  • Not a systematic review: no protocol, no risk-of-bias scoring, one reviewer, AI-assisted.

Findings

  • Hormones shift through the cycle Oestrogen rises through the follicular phase and peaks just before ovulation. After ovulation, progesterone becomes the main hormone1, with a smaller second rise in oestrogen mid-luteal4. Both are lowest in the days just before a period3.
  • The research is thin A 2024 scoping review found 26 studies. Most had fewer than 20 women, and most measured skin temperature, blood flow or sweating. None looked at acne1.
  • Irritation and water loss: small differences In 29 women, a skin irritant caused a stronger reaction on day 1 than on days 9–112. One study found skin lost more water just before a period than just before ovulation, measured on the back and forearm3. In 36 women, skin lost more water and held less a week after ovulation than at ovulation, but their own ratings of dryness, itch and sensitivity didn't change significantly4,5. The only facial moisture study in the 2024 review (51 women) found no change across phases1.
  • Premenstrual acne flares are common, not universal 44% of 400 women with acne reported flares before their period6. 63% of untreated adult women showed a 25% premenstrual rise in inflamed spots7. 65% of 105 women said their acne was worse around their period8. Many don't flare at all.
  • The ovulation "glow" isn't visible In 22 women, facial redness rose around ovulation and stayed up afterwards, but the change was too small for the human eye to detect, even in ideal lighting9.
  • The combined pill changes the picture It works by stopping ovulation and can help acne10. In the 400-woman survey, taking the pill made no difference to how often women had premenstrual flares6.
Overall

Studies show small average differences between phases, in small groups, and they sometimes disagree1,4. We found no study testing whether switching skincare products by phase improves skin. So the app treats its phase advice as timing tips, not rules.

What this changed in the app

  • Cut: honey and aloe vera, because the evidence didn't hold up. Ice on a deep spot was replaced with the AAD's warm-compress advice.
  • Replaced: "drier and duller during your period", with the supported finding that skin may react more easily around day 12.
  • Moved to myths: the "ovulation glow"9.
  • Hedged: headlines say "may" and "a little"; acne flares are given as a 44–65% range6–8; a note on the pill was added10.
  • Corrected on 27 Sep: two citations pointed at the wrong study, and four claims were a little stronger than their sources. All fixed, and every tip now links to at least one source.

Limits

  • The search wasn't exhaustive: it started from the sources the app already cited, so relevant studies may be missing.
  • The studies are small, measure different body sites on different cycle days, and the acne figures mostly come from surveys.
  • Four studies2,3,6,7 were read as abstracts only.
  • No dermatologist or pharmacist has reviewed this yet.
